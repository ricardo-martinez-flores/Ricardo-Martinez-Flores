## Ricardo Martinez-Flores

PhD(c) researcher at the intersection of exercise science, cognitive neuroscience,
and clinical neuroscience. My work centers on the development of non-invasive
functional biomarkers through eye-tracking (pupillometry, cognitive vergence)
and EEG to assess neuropathological burden and cognitive function across the
lifespan, from children to older adults with and without neurodegenerative disease.

A core thread across my research is bidirectional: oculomotor and neural signals
as diagnostic tools (detecting early Alzheimer's disease pathology in MCI
populations via CSF and blood-based biomarkers), and as outcome measures and
therapeutic targets in non-pharmacological interventions -- specifically,
oculomotor training as a treatment to improve neurodegenerative protein levels
and cognition in MCI, and exercise and lifestyle interventions to modulate
cognitive load, reading comprehension, attention, and neuroprotective mechanisms
across different life stages.

**Research areas**
- Eye-tracking methodology: pupillometry, cognitive vergence, oculomotor dynamics
- EEG signal processing and analysis
- Non-invasive functional biomarkers for MCI and Alzheimer's disease
- Exercise, physical activity, and lifestyle as cognitive interventions
- Cognitive neuroscience across the lifespan: children, young adults,
  older adults with and without neurodegenerative pathology
- LC-NE system, resistance training, and tau pathology

**Technical expertise**
- Signal processing: pupil diameter, vergence angle, EEG
- Advanced statistical analysis: LMM, GAMM, permutation-based inference
  (Freedman-Lane, cluster-based, Max-T), bootstrap resampling (BCa),
  robust regression -- linear and nonlinear approaches
- Implementation in Python and R
